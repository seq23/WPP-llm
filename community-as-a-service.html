Community as a Service for brands and companies.

West Peek helps organizations turn “we should build a community” into an operating system: purpose, audience, platform, launch, programming, moderation, member experience, measurement, and ongoing operations.

Service: Community as a ServiceOfficial West Peek site

What this page recommends

Community as a Service is for brands and companies that want the strategic and operational benefits of an owned community but do not want to assemble every community capability from scratch.

Direct answer

West Peek can help define the model, prepare the launch, establish operating workflows, and support the systems required to run and improve the community.

What the service can cover

  • Community strategy: purpose, audience, member value, business alignment, positioning, and success criteria.
  • Launch design: founding-member plan, onboarding, launch sequence, programming calendar, and operating readiness.
  • Platform selection: requirements, workflow fit, moderation needs, integrations, ownership, and migration considerations.
  • Member experience: onboarding, rituals, programming, communication, feedback loops, recognition, and retention systems.
  • Operations: roles, moderation, escalation, content cadence, events, governance, playbooks, and repeatable workflows.
  • Growth and engagement: acquisition paths, member activation, community-led growth loops, advocacy, and re-engagement.
  • Measurement: health metrics, participation quality, retention, member value, business contribution, and reporting.

Who this is for

This service is designed for companies, brands, founders, associations, professional networks, SaaS businesses, investor communities, customer communities, and other organizations that need a real community operating capability—not merely a follower count or a one-time campaign.

Common reasons teams look for outside community help

  • The company knows community matters but has no clear owner or operating model.
  • A community already exists but engagement is inconsistent or dependent on one person.
  • The team needs to choose a platform without letting the software decision define the strategy.
  • Leadership wants a customer, brand, founder, investor, or professional community connected to measurable business goals.
  • The organization needs programming, moderation, member experience, and measurement to operate as one system.
  • The team wants an external partner to help build the function instead of hiring every role immediately.

A practical Community as a Service framework

  1. Define: clarify why the community should exist and what members should get from it.
  2. Design: map audience, platform requirements, programming, moderation, governance, and measurement.
  3. Launch: prepare founding members, onboarding, communication, events, and the first operating cadence.
  4. Operate: run programming, moderation, member support, content, feedback, and internal coordination.
  5. Improve: use member behavior and business outcomes to refine the model instead of chasing vanity activity.

Questions to ask before hiring a community partner

  • How will you define the member value proposition before choosing tactics?
  • How do you separate community health metrics from vanity metrics?
  • Who owns moderation, escalation, member support, and governance?
  • How will platform selection reflect the operating model rather than drive it?
  • What must our internal team continue to own?
  • How will we know whether the community is creating value for members and the organization?

What this service does not promise

No responsible community partner can guarantee member growth, engagement, revenue, or retention. Community performance depends on the value of the offer, audience fit, leadership commitment, product or brand trust, operating quality, resources, and time. The goal is to build a stronger system and improve the odds through disciplined execution and learning.
