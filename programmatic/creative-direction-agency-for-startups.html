How to compare options

The sequence below is the selection sequence for creative direction agency work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  3. Test evidence against the exact use case. Ask each option how it handles approval bottlenecks, and compare the answers rather than the decks.

Where the selection usually goes wrong

  • Brief drift: name the signal that says brief drift has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • No asset governance: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Beautiful but unclear work: put the check in front of the commitment on creative direction agency work, rather than after it.
  • Approval bottlenecks: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • One-format thinking: rehearse the fallback against a real creative direction agency case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Startups

For startups, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of creative direction agency for startups worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
