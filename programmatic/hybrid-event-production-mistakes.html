Prevention controls

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up is a reason to narrow hybrid event production work, not to produce more of it. Leaving roles, run of show, rehearsal, and escalation unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once weak audience follow-through is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the controls are working

Measure hybrid event production at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means backup-path readiness as the leading signal and rehearsal completion as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need the earliest signal that would have shown the problem, not the eventual symptom, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

The failure modes to design against

  • Weak audience follow-through: name the signal that says weak audience follow-through has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Late assets: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear ownership: put the check in front of the commitment on hybrid event production work, rather than after it.
  • Unrehearsed speakers: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Single points of failure: rehearse the fallback against a real hybrid event production case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
