Operating approach

The sequence below is the decision sequence for community platform selection work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. State the desired outcome. Write the decision on community purpose, audience, and member value down, with the owner beside it.
  2. Identify owners. Name the evidence that would settle it, and where repeat participation comes from.
  3. Surface constraints. State the constraint that is real, and the one that is only engagement without operating ownership in disguise.
  4. Define evidence and measurement. Set the point at which engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth gets revisited rather than assumed.

Proof and measurement

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation is a reason to narrow community platform selection work, not to produce more of it. Leaving operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once unclear member value is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

Where this usually goes wrong

  • Unclear member value: name the signal that says unclear member value has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Engagement without operating ownership: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Platform-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community platform selection work, rather than after it.
  • Vanity membership counts: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No moderation or escalation model: rehearse the fallback against a real community platform selection case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Startups

For startups, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of community platform selection for startups worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
