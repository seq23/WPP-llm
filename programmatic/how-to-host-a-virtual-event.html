Virtual event checklist: how to host a virtual event, step by step

A virtual event checklist is the ordered list of decisions and tasks that takes an online event from idea to replay: goal and audience, date and platform, speakers and run of show, registration and promotion, rehearsal and backups, the live show, then recording, follow-up and reporting. Start six to eight weeks out for a summit.

At a glance
  • Lead time: six to eight weeks for a summit or conference, three to four weeks for a webinar.
  • One full rehearsal on the real platform two to five days before, and a technical check 60 minutes before going live.
  • Send the replay within 24 to 48 hours, while attendees still remember the event.
  • Moderated webinars: $2,500 to $7,500 per production (West Peek Productions published range, directional, not a fixed quote).

What goes on a virtual event checklist?

  1. Write the goal and the one number that says it worked (registrations, attendance, pipeline, sign-ups).
  2. Define the audience and the time zone you are planning for.
  3. Pick the format: webinar, panel, multi-session summit or hybrid.
  4. Set the date, avoiding holidays and competing events.
  5. Choose the platform against audience size, interaction and integrations.
  6. Confirm speakers and moderator, with a short brief for each.
  7. Draft the run of show minute by minute, with an owner for every cue.
  8. Build the registration page and confirmation emails.
  9. Plan promotion: email, social, partners and speaker posts, with dates.
  10. Collect slides and videos by a firm deadline.
  11. Design branded slides, lower thirds and holding screens.
  12. Write the Q&A and poll plan, including two seeded questions.
  13. Arrange captions and accessibility needs.
  14. Set up a backup: a second presenter machine, a second connection and a backup stream link.
  15. Send speaker kits: camera, microphone, lighting and joining instructions.
  16. Run a technical check with every speaker on their own setup.
  17. Hold a full rehearsal on the real platform, two to five days before.
  18. Send reminders one day and one hour before.
  19. Open the backstage 60 minutes before and test audio, video and slides.
  20. Run the show to the run of show, with one person calling cues.
  21. Moderate chat and Q&A, and keep time.
  22. Record the session and note timestamps for clips.
  23. Send the replay and resources within 24 to 48 hours.
  24. Follow up with attendees and no-shows separately.
  25. Report against the goal, and write down what to change next time.

How do you host a virtual event on the day?

  • One producer calls cues; speakers only present.
  • Keep a written run of show open for everyone backstage.
  • Have a moderator for chat and Q&A, separate from the host.
  • Start on time with a holding screen and music, not a silent room.
  • Know in advance who switches to the backup if the main stream fails.

How do you host a virtual event?

Set a goal, pick the format and platform, confirm speakers, write a run of show, open registration and promote it, rehearse on the real platform, run the show with one person calling cues and a moderator on Q&A, then send the replay and follow up within 48 hours.

What is a good virtual event planning timeline?

Six to eight weeks for a summit: lock goal, platform and date first, speakers and registration by week five, run of show and assets by week three, technical checks in the final week and a full rehearsal two to five days before. A webinar needs three to four weeks.

What goes on a virtual event production checklist?

The production items: run of show with cue owners, platform and registration setup, branded graphics, speaker kits and technical checks, captions, backup machine, connection and stream, a full rehearsal, recording and edited replay.

What is the best way to host a virtual event?

Separate the roles: a host who presents, a producer who calls cues, and a moderator who handles chat and Q&A. Rehearse on the real platform and agree in advance who switches to the backup if something fails.
