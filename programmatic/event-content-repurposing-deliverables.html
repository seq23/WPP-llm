Acceptance criteria

The sequence below is the deliverables sequence for event content repurposing work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Define format and owner. Write the boundary where audience outcome and format is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Define review standard. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on run-of-show ownership.
  3. Define dependencies. Say who owns the wording, because platform, broadcast, accessibility, and backup paths will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. Define final handoff. Check it against late assets: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure event content repurposing at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means run-of-show ownership as the leading signal and audience participation as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • Single points of failure: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear ownership: put the check in front of the commitment on event content repurposing work, rather than after it.
  • Late assets: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
