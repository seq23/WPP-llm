What is community programming?

A definition guide to community programming: what community purpose, audience, and member value has to settle, the evidence to require before committing, the early warning on engagement without operating ownership, and repeat participation as the check that the boundary is holding.

Direct answer

Settle community purpose, audience, and member value first and write down what it excludes; a community programming scope that cannot rule anything out will be read differently by everyone who inherits it. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what engagement without operating ownership would cost to fix late.

Definition and boundary

What is community programming is one decision inside community programming, and the job on this page is the narrow one: draw the boundary precisely enough that two people reading it would scope the same work. Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with community purpose, audience, and member value. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation is still open, because an operating model has to hold on its worst week rather than on its first.

What belongs inside the scope

The sequence below is the definition sequence for community programming work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Write the term in plain language. Write the boundary where community purpose, audience, and member value is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Name the inputs and outputs. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on repeat participation.
  3. Separate adjacent concepts. Say who owns the wording, because operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. State the decision the definition supports. Check it against vanity membership counts: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

What does not belong inside the scope

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on community purpose, audience, and member value is a reason to narrow community programming work, not to produce more of it. Leaving platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once engagement without operating ownership is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure community programming at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means repeat participation as the leading signal and business-aligned outcomes as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • Engagement without operating ownership: name the signal that says engagement without operating ownership has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Vanity membership counts: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Platform-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community programming work, rather than after it.
  • Unclear member value: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No moderation or escalation model: rehearse the fallback against a real community programming case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
