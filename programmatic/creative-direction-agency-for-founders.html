How to compare options

The sequence below is the selection sequence for creative direction agency work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Rank criteria before looking at vendors. Rank this against business objective and audience before any candidate is in the room.
  2. Separate must-haves from preferences. Score every option the same way, using approval cycle time as the comparable.
  3. Test evidence against the exact use case. Ask each option how it handles approval bottlenecks, and compare the answers rather than the decks.
  4. Name the decision owner. Record why the leader leads on distribution and reuse, in a sentence someone can disagree with.

Decision rule

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on asset plan, production constraints, and approvals is a reason to narrow creative direction agency work, not to produce more of it. And once brief drift is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

Where the selection usually goes wrong

  • Beautiful but unclear work: put the check in front of the commitment on creative direction agency work, rather than after it.
  • One-format thinking: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
