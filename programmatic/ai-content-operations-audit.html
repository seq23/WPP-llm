Evidence to collect

The sequence below is the audit sequence for AI content operations work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Define the standard first. Record the earliest signal that automating an unclear process has started, and who acts on it.
  2. Sample actual artifacts. Check this against the actual artifact for inputs, tools, owners, and permissions, not against a summary of it.
  3. Separate symptoms from root causes. Separate the symptom from the cause, and note which one human correction rate would have shown first.
  4. Rank by business impact and reversibility. Rank the finding by what it costs to fix and by how much of measurement, logs, and rollback is still open.

How to prioritize findings

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on inputs, tools, owners, and permissions is a reason to narrow AI content operations work, not to produce more of it. Leaving human checkpoints and exception handling unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once automation without evaluation is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the controls are working

Measure AI content operations at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means reliable rollback as the leading signal and time saved as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need the earliest signal that would have shown the problem, not the eventual symptom, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

The failure modes to design against

  • Automation without evaluation: name the signal that says automation without evaluation has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Automating an unclear process: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Silent failure: put the check in front of the commitment on AI content operations work, rather than after it.
  • Private-data leakage: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No owner: rehearse the fallback against a real AI content operations case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
