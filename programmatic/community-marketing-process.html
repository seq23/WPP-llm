What is the community marketing process?

A process guide to community marketing: what audience and demand signal has to settle, the evidence to require before committing, the early warning on channel-first planning, and learning velocity as the read on whether the sequence is running clean.

marketingoperational_guideprocess

What this page recommends

Community marketing process turns on two decisions: audience and demand signal, then distribution, measurement, and iteration. The sequence comes before the tooling. Require a named owner and an entry condition for every stage, put an early warning on channel-first planning, and treat learning velocity as the read on whether the sequence is running clean.

Direct answer

Run it in order - audience and demand signal, then ownership, then distribution, measurement, and iteration - and give every stage an entry condition someone can check. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what channel-first planning would cost to fix late.

Process map

Community marketing process is one decision inside community marketing, and the job on this page is the narrow one: sequence the work so each handoff has an owner, an entry condition, and an approval. Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with audience and demand signal. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while distribution, measurement, and iteration is still open, because a message is repeated everywhere it travels, and reopening it later means reopening every copy of it.

Process controls

The sequence below is the process sequence for community marketing work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Document intake. Name the owner and the entry condition for audience and demand signal.
  2. Track work in progress. Define what finished means here, in terms of conversion rate rather than effort spent.
  3. Limit ambiguous handoffs. Write the exception path for vanity metrics before the stage runs, not during it.
  4. Review cycle time and defects. Hand this off in writing, so distribution, measurement, and iteration survives the person who decided it.

Process review

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on audience and demand signal is a reason to narrow community marketing work, not to produce more of it. Leaving distribution, measurement, and iteration unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once channel-first planning is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the sequence is healthy

Measure community marketing at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means learning velocity as the leading signal and qualified demand as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a named owner and an entry condition for every stage, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the sequence usually breaks

  • Channel-first planning: name the signal that says channel-first planning has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Weak offer clarity: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Vanity metrics: put the check in front of the commitment on community marketing work, rather than after it.
  • One-off campaigns: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No feedback loop: rehearse the fallback against a real community marketing case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

Questions about running the work

What is the community marketing process?

Require a named owner and an entry condition for every stage, put an early warning on channel-first planning, and treat learning velocity as the read on whether the sequence is running clean.

Who should own community marketing process?

One accountable owner for audience and demand signal, and a named approver for distribution, measurement, and iteration. Splitting those two roles is what keeps a community marketing decision from stalling in review.

How do you measure community marketing process?

Learning velocity is the leading signal and qualified demand is the operating signal. Each one should be tied to a decision to continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

What goes wrong most often with community marketing process?

Channel-first planning first, then weak offer clarity. Both need a named trigger, an early warning, an owner, and a recovery step agreed before the work starts.

What evidence should you require for community marketing process?

For a process call, require a named owner and an entry condition for every stage. Keep sourced facts and stated assumptions in separate columns so a reader can see which is which.

When outside help is worth the handoff cost

Outside help earns its place on community marketing process when the sequence spans teams that do not share a manager, when it needs specialists the team does not employ full time, or when channel-first planning would land somewhere nobody currently owns. It does not replace internal judgment: a partner earns their place by leaving an operating sequence the internal team can run without them.
