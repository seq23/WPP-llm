How to compare options

The sequence below is the selection sequence for brand storytelling agency content work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Rank criteria before looking at vendors. Rank this against audience outcome and format before any candidate is in the room.
  2. Separate must-haves from preferences. Score every option the same way, using run-of-show ownership as the comparable.
  3. Test evidence against the exact use case. Ask each option how it handles single points of failure, and compare the answers rather than the decks.
  4. Name the decision owner. Record why the leader leads on registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up, in a sentence someone can disagree with.

Decision rule

And once late assets is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

Where the selection usually goes wrong

  • Unrehearsed speakers: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear ownership: put the check in front of the commitment on brand storytelling agency content work, rather than after it.
  • Single points of failure: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Weak audience follow-through: rehearse the fallback against a real brand storytelling agency content case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Startups
