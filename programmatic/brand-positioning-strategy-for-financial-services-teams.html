Ownership boundaries

The sequence below is the services sequence for brand positioning strategy work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Name each deliverable. Write the boundary where audience and category is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Assign owner and approver. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on sales-team adoption.
  3. Identify dependencies. Say who owns the wording, because message hierarchy and proof will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. Define what completion means. Check it against design before strategy: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

Service-level proof

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on message hierarchy and proof is a reason to narrow brand positioning strategy work, not to produce more of it. Leaving identity, channels, and rollout unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once unsupported claims is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure brand positioning strategy at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means sales-team adoption as the leading signal and category clarity as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • Unsupported claims: name the signal that says unsupported claims has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Design before strategy: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Generic positioning: put the check in front of the commitment on brand positioning strategy work, rather than after it.
  • Inconsistent language: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No adoption plan: rehearse the fallback against a real brand positioning strategy case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Financial Services Teams

For financial services teams, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of brand positioning strategy for financial services teams worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
