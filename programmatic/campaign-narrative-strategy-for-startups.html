Operating approach

The sequence below is the decision sequence for campaign narrative strategy work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. State the desired outcome. Write the decision on audience tension and desired belief down, with the owner beside it.
  2. Identify owners. Name the evidence that would settle it, and where proof density comes from.
  3. Surface constraints. State the constraint that is real, and the one that is only too many messages in disguise.
  4. Define evidence and measurement. Set the point at which editorial ownership and reuse gets revisited rather than assumed.

Measure campaign narrative strategy at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Where this usually goes wrong

  • Emotion without proof: name the signal that says emotion without proof has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Story without a business purpose: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Too many messages: put the check in front of the commitment on campaign narrative strategy work, rather than after it.
  • Founder-only context: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No repeatable narrative: rehearse the fallback against a real campaign narrative strategy case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Startups

For startups, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of campaign narrative strategy for startups worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
