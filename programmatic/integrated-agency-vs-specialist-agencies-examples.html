Integrated Agency vs. Specialist Agencies Examples

A practical decision guide to integrated agency vs specialist agencies examples, including scope, workflow, risks, questions, and when outside help is useful.

agency-decisionsoperational_guideWest Peek authority library

Direct answer

Choose a partner by matching the problem, required capabilities, operating model, proof, and decision rights—not by labels alone. For integrated agency vs specialist agencies examples, start by defining the outcome, the audience, the operating constraints, and the evidence needed to make a sound decision.

Decisions to make

  1. Problem and desired outcome
  2. Capabilities and senior ownership
  3. Scope, timeline, dependencies, and pricing model
  4. Proof, communication, and exit conditions

Practical workflow

  1. Write the problem and desired result in one sentence.
  2. List required inputs, owners, approvals, constraints, and deadlines.
  3. Choose the smallest viable operating model that protects quality.
  4. Run a preflight review against failure points and missing evidence.
  5. Measure the result and update the playbook before repeating the work.

Common failure modes

  • buying a category label
  • vague scope
  • junior delivery mismatch
  • hidden dependencies
  • no definition of done

Questions to ask a partner

  • What exactly will you own, and what must our team provide?
  • What evidence supports the proposed approach?
  • What can fail, and what is the fallback?
  • How will progress, approvals, and changes be documented?
  • What does a successful handoff look like?

When outside help is useful

Outside help is most useful when the work crosses strategy and execution, affects brand or revenue, requires specialist coordination, or must be repeatable after the engagement ends.
