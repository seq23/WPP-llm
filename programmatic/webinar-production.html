Webinar production services: what is included and what it costs

Webinar production services plan and run a webinar so the host only has to present: platform setup, run of show, speaker briefing and rehearsal, branded slides and graphics, live cueing, Q&A and polls, a backup plan, then the recording edit and follow-up assets. West Peek Productions produces moderated webinars from $2,500 to $7,500.

At a glance
  • Moderated webinars: $2,500 to $7,500 per production (West Peek Productions published range, directional, not a fixed quote).
  • Allow two to three weeks from brief to live; a repeat format can move faster.
  • Produced on the platform you already use, such as Zoom Webinars, Microsoft Teams, ON24 or StreamYard.
  • Deliverables: run of show, speaker brief and rehearsal, branded slide template and lower thirds, live producer, Q&A moderation, backup stream, edited recording and clips.

What do webinar production services include?

PhaseDeliverables
Pre-productionRun of show, speaker brief, slide review, branded templates and lower thirds, registration and reminder emails
RehearsalA technical check with each speaker on their own setup and one full rehearsal on the real platform
LiveA producer calling cues, Q&A and chat moderation, polls, timekeeping and a backup stream ready to switch
Post-productionEdited recording, short clips, attendee and no-show follow-up, and a results report

How does the webinar production process work?

  1. Brief call: goal, audience, speakers, date and platform.
  2. Run of show and creative: minute-by-minute plan, slides and branded graphics.
  3. Platform and registration setup, with reminder emails.
  4. Speaker technical checks and a full rehearsal.
  5. Live production with a producer and a moderator.
  6. Replay, clips, follow-up and a short report within 48 hours.

How much do webinar production services cost?

West Peek Productions publishes $2,500 to $7,500 for a moderated webinar production. These ranges are directional, not fixed quotes. What moves the price:

  • Number of speakers and whether they need coaching.
  • Custom graphics, video packages or a branded studio look.
  • Live captions or interpretation.
  • Length, and whether it is a one-off or a series.
  • Edited clips and repurposed content afterwards.

When should you outsource webinar production?

  • The webinar has commercial stakes: a launch, a sales pipeline, investors or sponsors.
  • There are three or more speakers, or external guests.
  • Your team would otherwise run the platform and present at the same time.
  • You run a series and want a repeatable format.

What does a webinar producer do?

A webinar producer owns everything the audience does not see: the run of show, speaker preparation, the platform backstage, cueing slides and videos, managing Q&A and polls, keeping time and switching to the backup if something fails. The speakers only present. For a longer plan, see the virtual event checklist.
