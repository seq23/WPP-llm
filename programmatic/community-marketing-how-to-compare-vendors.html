Direct answer

Rank the criteria before any candidate is in the room: distribution, measurement, and iteration first, then audience and demand signal, then whatever the shortlist wants to talk about. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what weak offer clarity would cost to fix late.

Vendor scorecard

Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with distribution, measurement, and iteration. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while audience and demand signal is still open, because a message is repeated everywhere it travels, and reopening it later means reopening every copy of it.

Reference checks

The sequence below is the vendor comparison sequence for community marketing work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Use the same questions. Rank this against audience and demand signal before any candidate is in the room.
  2. Score proof relevance. Score every option the same way, using conversion rate as the comparable.
  3. Test senior ownership. Ask each option how it handles vanity metrics, and compare the answers rather than the decks.
  4. Document why the winner won. Record why the leader leads on distribution, measurement, and iteration, in a sentence someone can disagree with.

Final selection

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on distribution, measurement, and iteration is a reason to narrow community marketing work, not to produce more of it. Leaving audience and demand signal unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once weak offer clarity is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the choice was right

Measure community marketing at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means cost to acquire attention as the leading signal and conversion rate as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need proof drawn from a situation close enough to this one to transfer, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the selection usually goes wrong

  • Weak offer clarity: name the signal that says weak offer clarity has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Vanity metrics: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Channel-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community marketing work, rather than after it.
  • One-off campaigns: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No feedback loop: rehearse the fallback against a real community marketing case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
