Webinar Production Services

What this page recommends

Webinar production services cover the technical and editorial work of running a live webinar: broadcast setup, rehearsal, live show-calling, speaker support, and recording delivery.

What is included in a production engagement

A production engagement is scoped around the run of show, not the software. It covers pre-production (format design, speaker briefing, dry run), live execution (broadcast control, lower thirds, speaker cueing, Q&A moderation support, failover monitoring), and post-production (recording cleanup, chaptering, asset handoff). The platform licence is usually yours; the operating capability is what you are buying.

Where the scope usually stops

Most engagements exclude audience acquisition, list building, and post-event nurture. They also exclude content authorship - a producer will shape a run of show and coach delivery, but will not write your subject-matter argument. Clarify slide production ownership early; it is the single most common scope dispute.

What a $2,500 webinar differs from a $7,500 one

Price moves with speaker count, rehearsal depth, and failover posture. A single-speaker session with one rehearsal and a straightforward deck sits at the low end. Multi-speaker panels, live demos, interpretation, or a redundant backup stream sit at the high end. Large executive summits and conferences are a different category and run $10,000 to $50,000 and above.

What goes wrong

The failures are rarely creative. They are an unrehearsed speaker joining from an untested network, a demo environment that was never load-checked, a moderator with no cue discipline, and no rehearsed failover path when a presenter drops. Ask any prospective producer what happens in the first thirty seconds after a speaker's connection dies - the answer separates operators from vendors.

How to decide whether you need this

If the webinar is internal, low-stakes, and single-speaker, your team can likely run it. Bring in production when the audience is external and the cost of a visible failure is high: analyst briefings, customer launches, executive appearances, regulated content, or anything being recorded for reuse.

Frequently asked questions

What do webinar production services cost?

West Peek produces moderated webinars in the $2,500 to $7,500 range, scaled by speaker count, rehearsal depth, and series versus single run. Executive summits, conferences, and hybrid productions are a separate category at $10,000 to $50,000 and above.

Do I need my own webinar platform?

Usually yes. The platform licence typically stays with you; production supplies the broadcast control, rehearsal, live operation, and continuity layer on top of it.

How far ahead should a webinar be booked?

Two to three weeks is workable for a straightforward single-speaker session. Multi-speaker panels and anything with a live demo need longer, because rehearsal availability rather than production capacity is the constraint.

Is a rehearsal really necessary?

For any external-facing session, yes. Most visible webinar failures trace to something that a dry run would have caught: an untested network, an unshared screen permission, or a demo that only works on one machine.
