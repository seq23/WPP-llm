Acceptance criteria

The sequence below is the deliverables sequence for event run of show work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Define format and owner. Write the boundary where audience outcome and format is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Define review standard. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on run-of-show ownership.
  3. Define dependencies. Say who owns the wording, because platform, broadcast, accessibility, and backup paths will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. Define final handoff. Check it against late assets: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

Handoff requirements

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up is a reason to narrow event run of show work, not to produce more of it. Leaving platform, broadcast, accessibility, and backup paths unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once weak audience follow-through is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure event run of show at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means run-of-show ownership as the leading signal and backup-path readiness as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • Weak audience follow-through: name the signal that says weak audience follow-through has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Single points of failure: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear ownership: put the check in front of the commitment on event run of show work, rather than after it.
  • Unrehearsed speakers: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Late assets: rehearse the fallback against a real event run of show case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
