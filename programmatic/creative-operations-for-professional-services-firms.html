What tells you the definition is holding

Measure creative operations at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means production predictability as the leading signal and brief adherence as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • One-format thinking: name the signal that says one-format thinking has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Brief drift: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Beautiful but unclear work: put the check in front of the commitment on creative operations work, rather than after it.
  • Approval bottlenecks: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No asset governance: rehearse the fallback against a real creative operations case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Professional Services Firms

For professional services firms, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of creative operations for professional services firms worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
