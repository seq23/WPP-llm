What should you decide first about community marketing?

A decision guide to community marketing: what audience and demand signal has to settle, the evidence to require before committing, the early warning on channel-first planning, and learning velocity as the signal that says whether this is working.

What this page recommends

Community marketing turns on two decisions: audience and demand signal, then offer and conversion path. The decision comes before the deliverable. Require a written record of what was assumed and what was observed, put an early warning on channel-first planning, and treat learning velocity as the signal that says whether this is working.

Direct answer

Take it in order - audience and demand signal, then ownership, then evidence - and leave tactics until the first three are settled. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what channel-first planning would cost to fix late.

Decision context

Community marketing is one decision inside community marketing, and the job on this page is the narrow one: turn the request into a small number of explicit decisions with evidence behind each one. Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with audience and demand signal. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while offer and conversion path is still open, because a message is repeated everywhere it travels, and reopening it later means reopening every copy of it.

Operating approach

The sequence below is the decision sequence for community marketing work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. State the desired outcome. Write the decision on audience and demand signal down, with the owner beside it.
  2. Identify owners. Name the evidence that would settle it, and where conversion rate comes from.
  3. Surface constraints. State the constraint that is real, and the one that is only vanity metrics in disguise.
  4. Define evidence and measurement. Set the point at which distribution, measurement, and iteration gets revisited rather than assumed.

Proof and measurement

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on audience and demand signal is a reason to narrow community marketing work, not to produce more of it. Leaving offer and conversion path unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once channel-first planning is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

Decision matrix for community marketing

What tells you this is working

Measure community marketing at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means learning velocity as the leading signal and conversion rate as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a written record of what was assumed and what was observed, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where this usually goes wrong

  • Channel-first planning: name the signal that says channel-first planning has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • No feedback loop: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Weak offer clarity: put the check in front of the commitment on community marketing work, rather than after it.
  • Vanity metrics: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • One-off campaigns: rehearse the fallback against a real community marketing case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

Who should own community marketing?

One accountable owner for audience and demand signal, and a named approver for offer and conversion path. Splitting those two roles is what keeps a community marketing decision from stalling in review.

How do you measure community marketing?

Learning velocity is the leading signal and conversion rate is the operating signal. Each one should be tied to a decision to continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

What goes wrong most often with community marketing?

Channel-first planning first, then no feedback loop. Both need a named trigger, an early warning, an owner, and a recovery step agreed before the work starts.

What evidence should you require for community marketing?

For a decision call, require a written record of what was assumed and what was observed. Keep sourced facts and stated assumptions in separate columns so a reader can see which is which.

When outside help is useful

Outside help earns its place on community marketing when the work crosses strategy and execution at the same time, when it needs specialists the team does not employ full time, or when channel-first planning would land somewhere nobody currently owns. It does not replace internal judgment: a partner earns their place by making the decision system clearer than they found it.
