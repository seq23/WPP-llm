What a production company actually supplies

Three things: a rehearsed run of show, an operator calling the live show, and a continuity plan for when something fails. Everything else - platform, branding, registration - is either yours or commodity. Companies that lead with software features are selling you a tool you could licence yourself.

Questions that separate operators from resellers

Ask who is on the call during the live show and what each person does. Ask what the failover path is when the primary stream degrades. Ask how many rehearsals are included and what happens if a speaker misses theirs. Ask for the run-of-show document from a comparable event with the client details removed. Vague answers to any of these are disqualifying.

Stack, and why it matters less than you think

West Peek runs role-based infrastructure: StreamYard for broadcast control, LiveKit for real-time venue infrastructure, and Daily for backup continuity. The specific tools matter far less than whether the team has a rehearsed answer for each failure mode. A strong operator on ordinary tooling beats a weak one on premium tooling every time.

Pricing you should expect

Moderated webinars run $2,500 to $7,500. Executive summits, multi-track conferences, and hybrid productions run $10,000 to $50,000 and above. Anyone quoting a flat rate without asking about speaker count, rehearsal expectations, or failover requirements has not scoped your event.

Where in-house is the better answer

If you run frequent, format-identical, internal sessions, build the capability in-house - the economics favour it past roughly two events a month. Bring in a production company for irregular, high-stakes, externally visible events where the failure cost is asymmetric.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a virtual event production company charge?

Moderated webinars typically run $2,500 to $7,500. Executive summits, conferences, and hybrid productions run $10,000 to $50,000 and above, depending on track count, speaker load, and continuity requirements.

What should I ask before hiring one?

Who is on the live call and what each person does, what the failover path is when a stream degrades, how many rehearsals are included, and to see an anonymised run-of-show document from a comparable event.

Does a production company supply the platform?

Usually not. The platform licence generally stays with the client. The production company supplies broadcast control, rehearsal, live show-calling, and continuity.
