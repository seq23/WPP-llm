Content marketing operations: the workflow behind every piece

Content marketing operations is the system that turns content requests into published, measured work: intake and briefs, a production calendar, writing and design, review and legal approval, publishing, distribution and reporting. AI content operations adds drafting, repurposing and tagging inside that workflow, with a human editor approving everything that ships.

At a glance
  • Seven stages, each with one owner and one output: intake, brief, production, review, approval, publishing, reporting.
  • Common tools: Asana, Monday.com or Airtable for the calendar; Google Docs or Notion for briefs; WordPress, Webflow or HubSpot for publishing; GA4 and HubSpot for reporting.
  • A realistic cycle for a long-form article is five to ten working days from approved brief to publish, most of it in review.

What does content marketing operations include?

  1. Intake: one request form, so work is not commissioned in chat threads.
  2. Brief: audience, goal, key message, format, owner and due date, approved before anyone writes.
  3. Production: writing, design and video on a shared calendar with capacity limits.
  4. Review: an editor checks against the brief, the style guide and a QA checklist.
  5. Approval: named approvers, including legal or compliance where needed, with a deadline for feedback.
  6. Publishing and distribution: a publishing checklist for SEO fields, links, tracking and channel versions.
  7. Reporting: monthly numbers on output, cycle time and what the content influenced.

What do content operation services deliver?

  • An intake form and a brief template.
  • An editorial calendar with owners and capacity.
  • A RACI that says who writes, reviews and approves.
  • A style guide and QA checklist.
  • A publishing checklist and a reuse plan for each long piece.
  • A reporting dashboard.
  • An AI usage policy: which steps may use AI, and who approves the output.

How do you measure content operations?

  • Cycle time from approved brief to published.
  • Throughput: pieces published per month against plan.
  • Revision rounds per piece.
  • On-time rate against the calendar.
  • Reuse: derivative assets per long-form piece.
  • Pipeline or sign-ups influenced by content.

What is content marketing operations?

What do content marketing operation services include?

Typically an intake form and brief template, an editorial calendar with owners, a RACI for review and approval, a style guide and QA checklist, a publishing checklist, a reporting dashboard and an AI usage policy, plus the people to run them if you hire an agency.

What is AI content operations?

AI content operations uses AI tools at named steps of the content workflow, such as research summaries, first drafts, repurposing and metadata, while an editor approves every output before it is published.

What does an agency content operations team do?

It designs the content workflow and runs it: intake, briefs, scheduling, production, review, publishing and reporting, with your team approving the work at agreed points.

What does a marketing content operation workflow look like?

Seven stages with one owner each: intake, brief, production, review, approval, publishing and distribution, and reporting. Work moves forward only when the previous stage's output is approved.
