Operating approach

Where this usually goes wrong

  • Late assets: name the signal that says late assets has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Unrehearsed speakers: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear ownership: put the check in front of the commitment on executive broadcast production work, rather than after it.
  • Single points of failure: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Weak audience follow-through: rehearse the fallback against a real executive broadcast production case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Enterprise Teams

For enterprise teams, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of executive broadcast production for enterprise teams worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
