What tells you the controls are working

Measure community building at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. The failure modes to design against

  • Unclear member value: name the signal that says unclear member value has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • No moderation or escalation model: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Platform-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community building work, rather than after it.
  • Engagement without operating ownership: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Vanity membership counts: rehearse the fallback against a real community building case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.
