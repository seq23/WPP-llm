Critical path

Creative strategy consulting timeline is one decision inside creative strategy consulting, and the job on this page is the narrow one: order the decisions around what cannot move rather than around a calendar. Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with creative proposition and references. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while asset plan, production constraints, and approvals is still open, because a live production is rehearsed before anyone judges it, and the decision needs rehearsal time as much as the delivery does.

Milestones and dependencies

The sequence below is the timeline sequence for creative strategy consulting work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Identify immovable dates. Fix the date business objective and audience is waiting on, and mark it as immovable or not.
  2. Work backward from approvals. Work backwards from the approval, not forwards from today, and hold time for approval cycle time.
  3. Reserve rehearsal or review time. Put the contingency in front of the irreversible step, where approval bottlenecks would otherwise land.
  4. Add contingency before irreversible steps. Say what happens to distribution and reuse if this date slips by a week.

Schedule risk

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on creative proposition and references is a reason to narrow creative strategy consulting work, not to produce more of it. Leaving asset plan, production constraints, and approvals unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once approval bottlenecks is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the schedule is real

Measure creative strategy consulting at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means approval cycle time as the leading signal and channel performance as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a dependency map showing what each date is waiting on, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the schedule usually fails

  • Approval bottlenecks: name the signal that says approval bottlenecks has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Brief drift: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Beautiful but unclear work: put the check in front of the commitment on creative strategy consulting work, rather than after it.
  • One-format thinking: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No asset governance: rehearse the fallback against a real creative strategy consulting case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
