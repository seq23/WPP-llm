Transferable lesson

And once vague scope is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What to measure, and against what baseline

Measure agency RFP questions at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means handoff quality as the leading signal and scope clarity as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a baseline taken before the work starts, so the change is attributable, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the measurement usually misleads

  • Vague scope: name the signal that says vague scope has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Junior delivery mismatch: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Buying a category label: put the check in front of the commitment on agency RFP questions work, rather than after it.
  • Hidden dependencies: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No definition of done: rehearse the fallback against a real agency RFP questions case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
