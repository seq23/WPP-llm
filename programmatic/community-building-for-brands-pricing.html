Start with platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. What changes the price

The sequence below is the pricing sequence for community building work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Normalize proposals to the same scope. Price it including the internal time community purpose, audience, and member value consumes.
  2. Identify assumptions and exclusions. Separate what is fixed from what varies with repeat participation, and say which assumption drives each.
  3. Ask how revisions are priced. Name the change that would move this number, and what engagement without operating ownership would cost if it landed late.
  4. Tie payment milestones to observable delivery. Tie a payment or approval to the observable completion of engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth.

How to compare proposals

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation is a reason to narrow community building work, not to produce more of it. Leaving community purpose, audience, and member value unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once engagement without operating ownership is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the money is working

Measure community building at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means member activation as the leading signal and member-to-member value as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need assumptions and exclusions written down beside the number, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the cost usually escapes

  • Engagement without operating ownership: name the signal that says engagement without operating ownership has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Platform-first planning: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear member value: put the check in front of the commitment on community building work, rather than after it.
  • Vanity membership counts: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No moderation or escalation model: rehearse the fallback against a real community building case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Brands

For brands, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of community building for brands pricing worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
