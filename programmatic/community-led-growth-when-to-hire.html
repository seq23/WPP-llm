Direct answer

Rank the criteria before any candidate is in the room: operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership first, then platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation, then whatever the shortlist wants to talk about. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what platform-first planning would cost to fix late.

Start with operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation is still open, because an operating model has to hold on its worst week rather than on its first.

Signals to stay internal

The sequence below is the hiring sequence for community-led growth work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Identify capacity constraint. Rank this against community purpose, audience, and member value before any candidate is in the room.
  2. Identify skill gap. Score every option the same way, using repeat participation as the comparable.
  3. Quantify delay cost. Ask each option how it handles engagement without operating ownership, and compare the answers rather than the decks.
  4. Define the smallest useful engagement. Record why the leader leads on engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth, in a sentence someone can disagree with.

Engagement trigger

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership is a reason to narrow community-led growth work, not to produce more of it. Leaving platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once platform-first planning is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the choice was right

Measure community-led growth at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means business-aligned outcomes as the leading signal and member-to-member value as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need proof drawn from a situation close enough to this one to transfer, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the selection usually goes wrong

  • Platform-first planning: name the signal that says platform-first planning has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • No moderation or escalation model: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear member value: put the check in front of the commitment on community-led growth work, rather than after it.
  • Engagement without operating ownership: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Vanity membership counts: rehearse the fallback against a real community-led growth case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

When to bring in an outside partner
