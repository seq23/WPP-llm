Acceptance criteria

The sequence below is the deliverables sequence for community management services work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Define format and owner. Write the boundary where community purpose, audience, and member value is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Define review standard. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on repeat participation.
  3. Define dependencies. Say who owns the wording, because operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. Define final handoff. Check it against vanity membership counts: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

Handoff requirements

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership is a reason to narrow community management services work, not to produce more of it. Leaving platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once no moderation or escalation model is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure community management services at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means retention as the leading signal and repeat participation as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • No moderation or escalation model: name the signal that says no moderation or escalation model has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Unclear member value: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Platform-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community management services work, rather than after it.
  • Engagement without operating ownership: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Vanity membership counts: rehearse the fallback against a real community management services case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
