How to compare options

The sequence below is the selection sequence for virtual summit production work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Rank criteria before looking at vendors. Rank this against audience outcome and format before any candidate is in the room.
  2. Separate must-haves from preferences. Score every option the same way, using run-of-show ownership as the comparable.
  3. Test evidence against the exact use case. Ask each option how it handles single points of failure, and compare the answers rather than the decks.
  4. Name the decision owner. Record why the leader leads on registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up, in a sentence someone can disagree with.

Decision rule

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up is a reason to narrow virtual summit production work, not to produce more of it. Leaving platform, broadcast, accessibility, and backup paths unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once weak audience follow-through is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.
