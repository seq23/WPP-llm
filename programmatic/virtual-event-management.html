Virtual event management: who owns what, from plan to replay

Virtual event management is running an online event as a project: goals and budget, platform choice, registration and promotion, speakers and content, the run of show, the live production, attendee support, and the follow-up and reporting. A virtual event manager owns the plan and the deadlines; a producer owns what happens live on the day.

At a glance
  • Eight workstreams, each with one owner (table below).
  • A summit needs six to eight weeks of management; a webinar three to four.
  • Typical tools: Zoom Events, Cvent or Bizzabo for the event; Eventbrite or Cvent for registration; Asana or Monday.com for the plan; HubSpot or Salesforce for follow-up.
  • Moderated webinars: $2,500 to $7,500 per production (West Peek Productions published range, directional, not a fixed quote).
  • Executive summits, online conferences and hybrid productions: $10,000 to $50,000 and above (West Peek Productions published range).

What does a virtual event manager do, week by week?

  1. Weeks eight to six: goal, budget, audience, format, date and platform.
  2. Weeks five to four: speakers confirmed, registration open, promotion running.
  3. Weeks three to two: run of show, branded assets, speaker kits, captions booked.
  4. Week one: speaker technical checks, rehearsal, reminders.
  5. Event day: backstage open early, attendee support, timekeeping.
  6. Week after: replay, follow-ups, report and lessons learned.

When should you bring in a virtual event management service?

  • No one on the team has run an online event of this size.
  • There are sponsors, several sessions or external speakers.
  • The event is tied to revenue, investors or a launch date.
  • The same people would otherwise manage, present and run the platform.

What else do people ask about virtual event management?

What is virtual event management?

Virtual event management is planning and running an online event as a project: goals, budget, platform, registration, promotion, speakers, run of show, live production, attendee support and follow-up, each with an owner and a deadline.

What does virtual events management include?

Eight workstreams: goals and budget, platform, registration and promotion, speakers and content, run of show, live production, attendee support, and follow-up and reporting.

What does a virtual event manager do?

The manager owns the plan: goals, budget, timeline, platform choice, promotion and follow-up, and makes sure every workstream has an owner. On the day, the producer runs the show and the manager handles decisions and attendee issues.

What are virtual events in event management?

Virtual events are events held online, such as webinars, summits, conferences and town halls. In event management they follow the same project discipline as in-person events, with the venue replaced by a platform and a production crew.
