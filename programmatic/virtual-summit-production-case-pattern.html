Decision pattern

The sequence below is the case pattern sequence for virtual summit production work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  4. State where the pattern may not transfer. Agree who reads audience participation, how often, and what they are expected to do about it.

Transferable lesson

What to measure, and against what baseline

Measure virtual summit production at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means post-event conversion or follow-through as the leading signal and rehearsal completion as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a baseline taken before the work starts, so the change is attributable, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the measurement usually misleads

  • Unclear ownership: name the signal that says unclear ownership has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Unrehearsed speakers: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Single points of failure: put the check in front of the commitment on virtual summit production work, rather than after it.
  • Late assets: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Weak audience follow-through: rehearse the fallback against a real virtual summit production case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
