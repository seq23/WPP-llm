Reference checks

The sequence below is the vendor comparison sequence for event content repurposing work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Use the same questions. Rank this against audience outcome and format before any candidate is in the room.
  2. Score proof relevance. Score every option the same way, using run-of-show ownership as the comparable.
  3. Test senior ownership. Ask each option how it handles single points of failure, and compare the answers rather than the decks.
  4. Document why the winner won. Record why the leader leads on registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up, in a sentence someone can disagree with.

Final selection

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on roles, run of show, rehearsal, and escalation is a reason to narrow event content repurposing work, not to produce more of it. Leaving registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once late assets is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

Where the selection usually goes wrong

  • Late assets: name the signal that says late assets has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Single points of failure: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear ownership: put the check in front of the commitment on event content repurposing work, rather than after it.
  • Unrehearsed speakers: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Weak audience follow-through: rehearse the fallback against a real event content repurposing case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
