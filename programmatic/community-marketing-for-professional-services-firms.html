Ownership boundaries

The sequence below is the services sequence for community marketing work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Name each deliverable. Write the boundary where audience and demand signal is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Assign owner and approver. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on conversion rate.
  3. Identify dependencies. Say who owns the wording, because campaign and content system will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. Define what completion means. Check it against one-off campaigns: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure community marketing at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means learning velocity as the leading signal and qualified demand as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • Vanity metrics: name the signal that says vanity metrics has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • One-off campaigns: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Channel-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community marketing work, rather than after it.
  • Weak offer clarity: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No feedback loop: rehearse the fallback against a real community marketing case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
