Evidence to collect

The sequence below is the audit sequence for creative strategy consulting work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Define the standard first. Record the earliest signal that beautiful but unclear work has started, and who acts on it.
  2. Sample actual artifacts. Check this against the actual artifact for creative proposition and references, not against a summary of it.
  3. Separate symptoms from root causes. Separate the symptom from the cause, and note which one asset reuse would have shown first.
  4. Rank by business impact and reversibility. Rank the finding by what it costs to fix and by how much of distribution and reuse is still open.

How to prioritize findings

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on creative proposition and references is a reason to narrow creative strategy consulting work, not to produce more of it. Leaving asset plan, production constraints, and approvals unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once approval bottlenecks is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the controls are working

Measure creative strategy consulting at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means brief adherence as the leading signal and approval cycle time as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need the earliest signal that would have shown the problem, not the eventual symptom, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

The failure modes to design against

  • Approval bottlenecks: name the signal that says approval bottlenecks has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Brief drift: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Beautiful but unclear work: put the check in front of the commitment on creative strategy consulting work, rather than after it.
  • One-format thinking: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No asset governance: rehearse the fallback against a real creative strategy consulting case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
