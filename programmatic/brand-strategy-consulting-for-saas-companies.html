How to compare options

The sequence below is the selection sequence for brand strategy consulting work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Rank criteria before looking at vendors. Rank this against audience and category before any candidate is in the room.
  2. Separate must-haves from preferences. Score every option the same way, using sales-team adoption as the comparable.
  3. Test evidence against the exact use case. Ask each option how it handles inconsistent language, and compare the answers rather than the decks.
  4. Name the decision owner. Record why the leader leads on identity, channels, and rollout, in a sentence someone can disagree with.

Decision rule

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on message hierarchy and proof is a reason to narrow brand strategy consulting work, not to produce more of it. Leaving positioning and differentiation unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once design before strategy is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

Where the selection usually goes wrong

  • Design before strategy: name the signal that says design before strategy has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • No adoption plan: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Generic positioning: put the check in front of the commitment on brand strategy consulting work, rather than after it.
  • Unsupported claims: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Inconsistent language: rehearse the fallback against a real brand strategy consulting case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for SaaS
