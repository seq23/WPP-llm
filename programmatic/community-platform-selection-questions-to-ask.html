Questions about proof

The sequence below is the questions sequence for community platform selection work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Ask who does the work. Record the earliest signal that platform-first planning has started, and who acts on it.
  2. Ask what evidence is relevant. Check this against the actual artifact for platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation, not against a summary of it.
  3. Ask what can fail. Separate the symptom from the cause, and note which one retention would have shown first.
  4. Ask how change is controlled. Rank the finding by what it costs to fix and by how much of engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth is still open.

Questions about delivery

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth is a reason to narrow community platform selection work, not to produce more of it. Leaving community purpose, audience, and member value unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once unclear member value is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the controls are working

Measure community platform selection at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means member-to-member value as the leading signal and member activation as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need the earliest signal that would have shown the problem, not the eventual symptom, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

The failure modes to design against

  • Unclear member value: name the signal that says unclear member value has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • No moderation or escalation model: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Platform-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community platform selection work, rather than after it.
  • Engagement without operating ownership: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Vanity membership counts: rehearse the fallback against a real community platform selection case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
