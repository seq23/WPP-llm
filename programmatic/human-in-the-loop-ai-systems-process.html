Direct answer

Process controls

The sequence below is the process sequence for human in the loop AI systems work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Document intake. Name the owner and the entry condition for workflow and failure cost.
  2. Track work in progress. Define what finished means here, in terms of exception rate rather than effort spent.
  3. Limit ambiguous handoffs. Write the exception path for private-data leakage before the stage runs, not during it.
  4. Review cycle time and defects. Hand this off in writing, so measurement, logs, and rollback survives the person who decided it.

What tells you the sequence is healthy

Measure human in the loop AI systems at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means human correction rate as the leading signal and traceability as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a named owner and an entry condition for every stage, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the sequence usually breaks

  • Automation without evaluation: name the signal that says automation without evaluation has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Silent failure: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Automating an unclear process: put the check in front of the commitment on human in the loop AI systems work, rather than after it.
  • Private-data leakage: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No owner: rehearse the fallback against a real human in the loop AI systems case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
