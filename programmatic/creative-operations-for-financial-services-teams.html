Ownership boundaries

The sequence below is the services sequence for creative operations work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Name each deliverable. Write the boundary where business objective and audience is decided, and name one thing it excludes.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure creative operations at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means channel performance as the leading signal and brief adherence as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • No asset governance: name the signal that says no asset governance has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Beautiful but unclear work: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Brief drift: put the check in front of the commitment on creative operations work, rather than after it.
  • Approval bottlenecks: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • One-format thinking: rehearse the fallback against a real creative operations case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How this changes for Financial Services Teams

For financial services teams, calibrate that to the team's real decision speed, internal depth, and stakeholder count rather than to a model borrowed from a much larger organisation. Decide which approvals are genuinely mandatory, which work can move asynchronously, and which evidence has to be retained once the people who made the decision have moved on.

The version of creative operations for financial services teams worth writing down is the one that survives turnover. Record the criteria, not only the choice, so the next operator can see what would justify changing it.
