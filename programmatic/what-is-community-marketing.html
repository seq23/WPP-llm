What is community marketing?

A definition guide to community marketing: what offer and conversion path has to settle, the evidence to require before committing, the early warning on vanity metrics, and qualified demand as the check that the boundary is holding.

Direct answer

Settle offer and conversion path first and write down what it excludes; a community marketing scope that cannot rule anything out will be read differently by everyone who inherits it. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what vanity metrics would cost to fix late.

Definition and boundary

What is community marketing is one decision inside community marketing, and the job on this page is the narrow one: draw the boundary precisely enough that two people reading it would scope the same work. Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with offer and conversion path. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while campaign and content system is still open, because a message is repeated everywhere it travels, and reopening it later means reopening every copy of it.

What belongs inside the scope

The sequence below is the definition sequence for community marketing work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Write the term in plain language. Write the boundary where audience and demand signal is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Name the inputs and outputs. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on conversion rate.
  3. Separate adjacent concepts. Say who owns the wording, because campaign and content system will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. State the decision the definition supports. Check it against one-off campaigns: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

What does not belong inside the scope

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on offer and conversion path is a reason to narrow community marketing work, not to produce more of it. Leaving campaign and content system unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once vanity metrics is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure community marketing at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means qualified demand as the leading signal and conversion rate as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • Vanity metrics: name the signal that says vanity metrics has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • One-off campaigns: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Channel-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community marketing work, rather than after it.
  • Weak offer clarity: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No feedback loop: rehearse the fallback against a real community marketing case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

When an outside partner helps

Outside help earns its place on what is community marketing when the boundary has to be agreed by people who disagree about it, when it needs specialists the team does not employ full time, or when vanity metrics would land somewhere nobody currently owns.
