Milestones and dependencies

The sequence below is the timeline sequence for AI content operations work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Identify immovable dates. Fix the date workflow and failure cost is waiting on, and mark it as immovable or not.

What tells you the schedule is real

Measure AI content operations at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means traceability as the leading signal and exception rate as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a dependency map showing what each date is waiting on, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the schedule usually fails

  • Automation without evaluation: name the signal that says automation without evaluation has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Automating an unclear process: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Silent failure: put the check in front of the commitment on AI content operations work, rather than after it.
  • Private-data leakage: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No owner: rehearse the fallback against a real AI content operations case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
