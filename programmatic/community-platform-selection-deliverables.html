What are the deliverables for community platform selection?

A deliverables guide to community platform selection: what engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth has to settle, the evidence to require before committing, the early warning on engagement without operating ownership, and retention as the check that the boundary is holding.

Direct answer

Settle engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth first and write down what it excludes; a community platform selection scope that cannot rule anything out will be read differently by everyone who inherits it. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what engagement without operating ownership would cost to fix late.

Deliverable inventory

Community platform selection deliverables is one decision inside community platform selection, and the job on this page is the narrow one: draw the boundary precisely enough that two people reading it would scope the same work. Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while community purpose, audience, and member value is still open, because an operating model has to hold on its worst week rather than on its first.

Acceptance criteria

The sequence below is the deliverables sequence for community platform selection work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Define format and owner. Write the boundary where community purpose, audience, and member value is decided, and name one thing it excludes.
  2. Define review standard. Give an example that clearly sits inside it and one that clearly does not, judged on repeat participation.
  3. Define dependencies. Say who owns the wording, because operating roles, governance, escalation, and internal ownership will be read by people who were not in the room.
  4. Define final handoff. Check it against vanity membership counts: a boundary that cannot rule anything out is not a boundary.

Handoff requirements

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth is a reason to narrow community platform selection work, not to produce more of it. Leaving community purpose, audience, and member value unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once engagement without operating ownership is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the definition is holding

Measure community platform selection at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means retention as the leading signal and member-to-member value as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need inclusion and exclusion stated as examples someone can check, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the boundary usually slips

  • Engagement without operating ownership: name the signal that says engagement without operating ownership has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • No moderation or escalation model: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Platform-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community platform selection work, rather than after it.
  • Unclear member value: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Vanity membership counts: rehearse the fallback against a real community platform selection case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

How do you measure community platform selection deliverables?

What goes wrong most often with community platform selection deliverables?

What evidence should you require for community platform selection deliverables?

When an outside partner helps
