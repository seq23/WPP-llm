What to measure, and against what baseline

Measure human in the loop AI systems at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means human correction rate as the leading signal and traceability as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a baseline taken before the work starts, so the change is attributable, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the measurement usually misleads

  • Private-data leakage: name the signal that says private-data leakage has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Automation without evaluation: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Automating an unclear process: put the check in front of the commitment on human in the loop AI systems work, rather than after it.
  • Silent failure: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • No owner: rehearse the fallback against a real human in the loop AI systems case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
