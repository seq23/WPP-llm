What is the community-led growth process?

A process guide to community-led growth: what engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth has to settle, the evidence to require before committing, the early warning on no moderation or escalation model, and member-to-member value as the read on whether the sequence is running clean.

What this page recommends

Community-led growth process turns on two decisions: engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth, then platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation. The sequence comes before the tooling. Require a named owner and an entry condition for every stage, put an early warning on no moderation or escalation model, and treat member-to-member value as the read on whether the sequence is running clean.

Direct answer

Run it in order - engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth, then ownership, then platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation - and give every stage an entry condition someone can check. How far to take each step depends on how reversible the commitment is, and on what no moderation or escalation model would cost to fix late.

Process map

Community-led growth process is one decision inside community-led growth, and the job on this page is the narrow one: sequence the work so each handoff has an owner, an entry condition, and an approval. Two people can search the same topic and need different evidence, so the useful move is to say which part is standard, which part is contingent, and what the reader has to inspect first-hand.

Start with engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth. Set down where things stand now, where they need to be, and which constraints are genuinely fixed. Keep the commitment reversible while platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation is still open, because an operating model has to hold on its worst week rather than on its first.

Process controls

The sequence below is the process sequence for community-led growth work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Document intake. Name the owner and the entry condition for community purpose, audience, and member value.
  2. Track work in progress. Define what finished means here, in terms of repeat participation rather than effort spent.
  3. Limit ambiguous handoffs. Write the exception path for engagement without operating ownership before the stage runs, not during it.
  4. Review cycle time and defects. Hand this off in writing, so engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth survives the person who decided it.

Process review

Tie the next move to what is actually known. Weak evidence on engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth is a reason to narrow community-led growth work, not to produce more of it. Leaving platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation unresolved is what lets scope grow without an owner or a date. And once no moderation or escalation model is visible, the honest move is a fallback or a smaller scope, before more money follows the plan.

What tells you the sequence is healthy

Measure community-led growth at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means member-to-member value as the leading signal and retention as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a named owner and an entry condition for every stage, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the sequence usually breaks

  • No moderation or escalation model: name the signal that says no moderation or escalation model has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Engagement without operating ownership: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Platform-first planning: put the check in front of the commitment on community-led growth work, rather than after it.
  • Unclear member value: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Vanity membership counts: rehearse the fallback against a real community-led growth case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.

Questions about running the work

What is the community-led growth process?

The sequence comes before the tooling. Who should own community-led growth process?

One accountable owner for engagement, retention, measurement, and community-led growth, and a named approver for platform, onboarding, programming, and moderation. Splitting those two roles is what keeps a community-led growth decision from stalling in review.

How do you measure community-led growth process?

Member-to-member value is the leading signal and retention is the operating signal. Each one should be tied to a decision to continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

What goes wrong most often with community-led growth process?

No moderation or escalation model first, then engagement without operating ownership. Both need a named trigger, an early warning, an owner, and a recovery step agreed before the work starts.

What evidence should you require for community-led growth process?

For a process call, require a named owner and an entry condition for every stage. Keep sourced facts and stated assumptions in separate columns so a reader can see which is which.

When outside help is worth the handoff cost

Outside help earns its place on community-led growth process when the sequence spans teams that do not share a manager, when it needs specialists the team does not employ full time, or when no moderation or escalation model would land somewhere nobody currently owns. It does not replace internal judgment: a partner earns their place by leaving an operating sequence the internal team can run without them.
