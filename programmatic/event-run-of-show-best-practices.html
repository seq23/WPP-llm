Quality gates

The sequence below is the best practices sequence for event run of show work, not a generic plan. Each step ends in something observable, so the next one starts from evidence rather than from momentum.

  1. Document the minimum viable standard. Name the owner and the entry condition for audience outcome and format.
  2. Assign owners. Define what finished means here, in terms of run-of-show ownership rather than effort spent.
  3. Use preflight gates. Write the exception path for single points of failure before the stage runs, not during it.
  4. Review evidence after each cycle. Hand this off in writing, so registration, engagement, recording, and follow-up survives the person who decided it.

What tells you the sequence is healthy

Measure event run of show at two levels: the outcome the work exists to change, and the operating signals that move first. Here that means audience participation as the leading signal and post-event conversion or follow-through as the one that shows whether the system underneath is healthy. Both need a named owner and an entry condition for every stage, and each should be attached to a decision - continue, narrow, change owner, or stop.

Where the sequence usually breaks

  • Single points of failure: name the signal that says single points of failure has begun, and the person expected to act on it.
  • Weak audience follow-through: write the recovery step while it is still a choice: who reduces scope, who tells the stakeholder, and what gets rehearsed.
  • Unclear ownership: put the check in front of the commitment on event run of show work, rather than after it.
  • Unrehearsed speakers: assign it to a named person rather than to a meeting, so it is not left to whoever notices first.
  • Late assets: rehearse the fallback against a real event run of show case at least once; an untested fallback is a plan, not a control.
