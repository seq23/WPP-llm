Are webinars live or recorded?

A webinar is a web-based seminar that can be delivered either live in real-time or as a pre-recorded session. The format depends entirely on your event goals, the need for audience interaction, and your team's capacity to manage unexpected technical issues during the broadcast.

Published 6 November 2026 · West Peek Productions

What is a live webinar?

A live webinar happens in real-time. Presenters speak, share slides, and interact with attendees at the exact moment the broadcast airs. This format creates a sense of immediacy and urgency. Attendees can ask questions through a chat window, and presenters can answer them on the spot. For town halls, product launches, and interactive training sessions, the live format is often the standard choice because it allows for direct engagement and immediate feedback.

Running a live event requires reliable internet connections, dedicated streaming hardware, and a run of show that accounts for potential delays. Your production team must manage microphones, lighting, slide transitions, and video feeds simultaneously. While the stakes are higher because mistakes happen in real-time, the energy of a live broadcast often keeps participants more attentive and invested in the content.

You should choose a live webinar when your event depends on community participation, live polling, or unscripted Q and A segments. It is also the right fit when information is breaking news or requires immediate discussion across your organization. Planning a live webinar means building in rehearsal time so every speaker feels comfortable with the technology and the broadcast schedule.

  • Real-time audience interaction via chat and Q and A
  • Higher engagement through live polls and surveys
  • Unscripted moments that feel authentic and urgent
  • Requires robust technical backup and stable internet

What is a pre-recorded webinar?

A pre-recorded webinar is filmed, edited, and produced before the scheduled broadcast date. When the event time arrives, the video file plays for the audience as if it were happening live. This approach removes the risk of technical glitches during the presentation. You can edit out stumbles, fix audio issues, add polished graphics, and ensure that your pacing is tight and professional.

Pre-recorded content allows your presenters to relax. They do not have to worry about missing cues or dealing with unexpected background noise. You can record sessions weeks in advance, secure approvals from legal or communications teams, and subtitle the video for accessibility. Many organizations use pre-recorded webinars for compliance training, standardized onboarding, and international events where time zones make live attendance difficult.

The main drawback of a purely pre-recorded session is the lack of genuine spontaneity. If your audience expects to ask questions and receive immediate answers, a static video will fall short. However, you can solve this by pairing a pre-recorded video with a live chat moderator who answers questions in real-time while the video plays on screen.

  • Fully edited video with polished graphics and sound
  • Zero risk of live speaker stumbles or tech dropouts
  • Easy to schedule across multiple global time zones
  • Allows for asynchronous viewing after the initial broadcast

What is a simulated live webinar?

A simulated live webinar combines the best elements of both formats. You record the presentation ahead of time to ensure flawless execution and professional quality. Then, you broadcast that recording at a set date and time as a scheduled event. While the video plays automatically, a team of hosts or subject matter experts monitors the chat to answer questions, run polls, and interact with viewers live.

This hybrid approach protects your speakers from live broadcast stress while still giving attendees the feeling of attending a real event. It is particularly useful for recurring webinars, global broadcasts that need to run multiple times a day, and events featuring busy executives who cannot commit to a specific live window. The audience gets a polished presentation alongside responsive human interaction.

When setting up a simulated live event, transparency is helpful. Let your audience know that the main presentation is pre-recorded but that speakers are present in the chat to answer questions. This manages expectations while maintaining high engagement. Your technical crew will manage the video playback platform and coordinate the live chat stream behind the scenes.

  • Pre-recorded presentation paired with real-time chat
  • Ideal for repeating broadcasts across global regions
  • Protects executive speakers from live technical errors
  • Keeps the audience engaged through active moderation

How to choose the right format for your event

Choosing between live, recorded, and simulated live webinars comes down to your content, your speakers, and your audience expectations. Start by looking at the core purpose of your event. If your goal is a high-stakes corporate town hall where employees can challenge leadership directly, a live broadcast is necessary. If your goal is a training module that employees must watch at their own pace, an on-demand recording is the correct tool.

Consider the technical readiness of your team and speakers. Live production demands calm execution under pressure and backup plans for internet failure. If your speakers are inexperienced or nervous on camera, recording their sessions in a controlled studio environment yields a better end product. You can always add live elements, such as a moderated question session, to the end of a recorded presentation.

Discuss your options with your production partner early in the planning process. A professional video production team can help you weigh the risks and benefits of each format based on your venue, your platform capabilities, and your schedule. They will ensure your chosen format supports your communication goals without overwhelming your internal staff.

  • Align the format with your specific event goals
  • Evaluate speaker comfort and technical readiness
  • Consider audience expectations for interaction
  • Consult production partners for platform capabilities

Frequently asked questions

Can attendees tell if a webinar is pre-recorded?

Attendees often cannot tell if a webinar is pre-recorded unless the platform explicitly labels it. High production quality, seamless editing, and active live chat moderation make simulated live events feel identical to real-time broadcasts for most viewers.

What equipment do I need for a live webinar?

A professional live webinar requires reliable hardware encoders, professional microphones, adequate lighting, stable high-speed internet with backups, and a dedicated streaming platform that can handle your expected attendee volume without crashing.

Are recorded webinars available on-demand?

Yes, most webinar platforms allow you to save the recording and publish it as an on-demand video. This lets registered attendees who missed the live broadcast watch the content later at their convenience.

How do I handle audience questions during a recorded webinar?

You can handle questions during a recorded webinar by pairing the video with a live text chat. Staff members or presenters monitor the chat while the video plays, answering questions in real-time and providing relevant links.
