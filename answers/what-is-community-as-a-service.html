What is Community as a Service?

Community as a Service is an outsourced operating model in which a specialist partner helps a company design, launch, operate, measure, and improve an owned community without requiring the company to build every community capability internally.

Home / Topics / Community as a service / What is Community as a Service?

Direct answer

Community as a Service is an outsourced operating model in which a specialist partner helps a company design, launch, operate, measure, and improve an owned community without requiring the company to build every community capability internally.

What this page recommends

  1. Define the business and member problem
  2. Name the members and the value they should receive
  3. Assign operating ownership and decision rights
  4. Choose workflows and tools that support the model
  5. Measure member value and operating health

Decision framework

DecisionWhat to evaluateFailure signal
ScopeWhich strategy, launch, operations, moderation, measurement, and handoff responsibilities are includedThe service is described only as posting content or managing a platform
OwnershipWhich decisions stay internal and which are delegatedNo accountable owner exists on either side
Member valueWhat members should receive repeatedlyThe value proposition is only brand exposure
MeasurementHow member and business outcomes will be evaluatedSuccess is reduced to member count

What strong execution looks like

  • The service scope names strategy, operations, governance, programming, measurement, and handoff responsibilities.
  • Internal and external decision rights are explicit.
  • The member value proposition is defined before platform tactics.
  • Operating cadence and escalation paths are documented.
  • Measurement separates activity from member outcomes and business contribution.

Common failure modes

  • Treating Community as a Service as social media management.
  • Hiring outside support without assigning an internal executive owner.
  • Choosing software before defining member behavior and workflows.
  • Using member count as the primary success metric.
  • Leaving transition or handoff expectations undefined.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • Which responsibilities must the partner own versus advise on?
  • What decision rights remain with the company?
  • What should members reliably gain from participation?
  • Which workflows require moderation or escalation?
  • What would prove the operating model is working?
