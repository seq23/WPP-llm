What this page recommends

Community as a Service pricing depends on scope: strategy only, launch support, ongoing operations, moderation, programming, analytics, platform administration, and the amount of execution the partner owns.

Direct answer

A responsible estimate starts with operating scope and decision rights rather than a one-size-fits-all package price.

How to approach it

  1. Define the scope the partner will own
  2. Separate strategy, launch, and ongoing operations
  3. List platform, programming, moderation, and analytics needs
  4. Clarify internal responsibilities and approvals
  5. Compare total operating cost, not just a monthly fee

Decision framework

DecisionWhat to evaluateFailure signal
ScopeStrategy-only, launch, ongoing operations, moderation, programming, analytics, or a combinationComparing fees without comparing included responsibility
ComplexityMember count, access model, moderation risk, integrations, programming, and stakeholder countAssuming every community has the same operating burden
Internal loadTime and skills the company must still provideIgnoring internal labor when comparing total cost
DurationLaunch project, transition period, or ongoing managed serviceUsing a short pilot price as a steady-state estimate

What strong execution looks like

  • Pricing is tied to a written responsibility map.
  • One-time launch costs are separated from recurring operations.
  • Internal labor and software costs are included in total-cost comparison.
  • Scope changes have explicit triggers.
  • The buyer can tell what is not included.

Common failure modes

  • Choosing the lowest monthly fee without comparing ownership.
  • Bundling platform fees and professional services into an opaque number.
  • Ignoring moderation, analytics, or event/programming workload.
  • Assuming a large existing community costs the same to operate as a new pilot.
  • Failing to budget transition and documentation.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • Which responsibilities drive most of the operating effort?
  • What is one-time versus recurring?
  • Which software or event costs are separate?
  • How much internal staff time remains required?
  • What would cause the scope or fee to change?
