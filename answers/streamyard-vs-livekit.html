StreamYard vs LiveKit for virtual events

StreamYard is better understood as a broadcast control layer for switching, overlays, and public streams; LiveKit is better understood as realtime venue infrastructure for interactive event rooms and custom attendee experiences.

Direct answer

StreamYard is better understood as a broadcast control layer for switching, overlays, and public streams; LiveKit is better understood as realtime venue infrastructure for interactive event rooms and custom attendee experiences.

StreamYard and LiveKit side by side

DimensionStreamYardLiveKit
How the vendor describes it“A professional live streaming and recording studio in your browser.”“The platform for voice, video, and physical AI agents”; the server repository describes “scalable, multi-user conferencing based on WebRTC.”
What you are givenA finished studio interface you operate. Nothing is assembled by you.A server and client SDKs you build an event experience on top of.
Published licenceCommercial hosted product; no server licence is published.Apache License 2.0, source at github.com/livekit/livekit.
Underlying architecture, as publishedNot published by the vendor.A Selective Forwarding Unit written in Go, built on the Pion WebRTC implementation.
Where the output goesOut to public platforms — the vendor names broadcasting live to several at once, including Facebook and YouTube.Into whatever room or application you build; there is no built-in public broadcast destination.
Question it answers on an event“What does the audience see, and who is on screen right now?”“What can an attendee do in the room, and who is allowed to do it?”
Role West Peek gives itBroadcast production control: switching, overlays, and public stream discipline.Realtime venue infrastructure: participant permissions, interaction, and room logic.

Vendor rows are taken from the vendors’ own pages, each fetched and checked on 27 August 2026: streamyard.com, docs.livekit.io and github.com/livekit/livekit. The last row is West Peek Productions’ own operating assignment, not a vendor claim. Where a vendor does not publish a figure, this table says so rather than estimating one.
