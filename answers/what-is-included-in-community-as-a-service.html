Direct answer

The exact scope should state what the external partner owns and what remains internal.

How to approach it

  1. Define purpose, member value, and business boundaries
  2. Map owners, workflows, programming, and moderation
  3. Launch with explicit operating cadences
  4. Measure member and operational health
  5. Document, improve, and hand off repeatable playbooks

Decision framework

DecisionWhat to evaluateFailure signal
Weekly cadenceRecurring moderation, support, programming, reporting, and planningImportant work happens only when someone remembers
OwnershipAccountable person for every recurring workflowTasks are shared but ownership is unclear
EscalationRules for exceptions, incidents, and executive decisionsEdge cases block routine operations
DocumentationPlaybooks, calendars, templates, decisions, and change historyCritical knowledge lives in one person’s head

What strong execution looks like

  • Recurring work runs on a visible operating cadence.
  • Owners and backup owners are named.
  • Routine work is separated from exceptions.
  • Playbooks are updated when reality changes.
  • Metrics and member feedback feed the next operating cycle.

Common failure modes

  • Running community entirely from a content calendar.
  • Letting moderation, support, and events use disconnected processes.
  • No backup when a key operator is unavailable.
  • Repeating manual work that could be standardized.
  • Collecting feedback without assigning action.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • What must happen daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly?
  • Who owns each recurring workflow?
  • Which exceptions need escalation?
  • What should be documented so another operator can step in?
  • Which operating metric should trigger a change?
