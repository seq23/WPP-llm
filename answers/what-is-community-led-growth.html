What is community-led growth, and does it work for a small business?

Community-led growth (CLG) is a growth strategy in which a business’s community of customers and fans drives how it finds, keeps and serves customers: members recommend it, answer each other’s questions and help shape what it makes. Shopify defines it as “a marketing strategy that prioritizes the opinions of customers” to influence product development and marketing.

Quick answer

Community-led growth means your customers, connected to each other, do much of the work of growing the business: recommending it, helping new customers and shaping the product. It can work for a small business when customers share a real reason to meet and the owner shows up regularly, but the published evidence is mostly case studies, so test it for a few quarters before you rely on it.

What this guide is best for: small business owners and marketing leads deciding whether to make a community part of how they grow.

What does community-led growth mean?

It means growth comes through the relationships between your customers, not only through your own marketing.

  • Customers find you through members: referrals, word of mouth and members inviting peers
  • Customers stay because of members: they get help, ideas and friendships they would lose by leaving
  • The product improves through members: their feedback and votes decide what comes next

The idea is older than the term. Harvard Business Review described Harley-Davidson’s brand community as central to its recovery after the company nearly collapsed in 1983 (Fournier and Lee, HBR, 2009).

How is it different from product-led and sales-led growth?

The difference is who does the convincing: the community, the product, or a sales team.

ModelWho wins the customerTypical first step
Community-ledOther customers, through recommendations, help and shared eventsJoin a group, attend an event, ask a question
Product-ledThe product itself, used before buyingA free trial or free plan
Sales-ledA salespersonA call or a demo

They are not exclusive. A community often helps a product-led or sales-led business by teaching customers and sending referrals.

What are examples of community-led growth?

The best-documented examples let the community change what the business makes or how it reaches people.

  • LEGO Ideas: fans post set designs and vote for each other’s; a design that reaches 10,000 supporters goes to LEGO review (LEGO), and the designer of a chosen set receives 1% of the royalties (Wikipedia).
  • Set Active: the activewear brand’s community votes on designs, with two community collections a year (Shopify).
  • Knix: when customers criticized a launch video, the brand pulled it and reshot it with messaging based on their feedback (Shopify).
  • Actively Black: the founder built an audience on Instagram, email and text before launching a product (Shopify).
  • Harley Owners Group: created by Harley-Davidson in 1983, with local chapters sponsored by dealerships (Wikipedia).

Results these brands report are their own and are not typical or guaranteed. For more examples and the step-by-step setup, see how to build a community around your business.

Does community-led growth work for a small business?

It can, and a small business has advantages a large one does not, but it is not right for every business.

Likely to work whenUnlikely to work when
Customers share an interest, problem or identity beyond the purchasePeople buy once and have no reason to meet again
The owner or a staff member can show up every week or monthNobody has time to host, so the space goes quiet
Customers already ask each other for adviceCustomers want a transaction, not a conversation
You can act on member feedback and show itDecisions cannot change, so feedback goes nowhere

The small business advantage: the founder can know members by name, meet them in person and act on an idea the same week. Shopify’s examples include XO Marshmallow, which started at farmers markets and kept that in-person approach after moving online (Shopify).

What the evidence is: most published proof is case studies written by brands or by companies that sell to them. We did not find controlled studies showing that community-led growth beats other strategies for small businesses, so measure your own results rather than expect someone else’s.

How do you start community-led growth in 90 days?

  1. Month 1: pick the purpose and the first members. One sentence on who it is for; invite your most engaged customers by hand.
  2. Month 1: choose one home and one rhythm, such as a monthly live session or a weekly thread.
  3. Month 2: give members a decision. A vote on a product, a topic for the next session, a member who hosts.
  4. Month 2: connect people. Introduce members to each other, not only to you.
  5. Month 3: measure. Who came back, who answered someone else, who referred a new customer.
  6. Month 3: decide who runs it next: you, a hire, or an agency (see community manager or community agency).

Frequently asked questions

What does community-led growth mean?

Community-led growth means a business grows mainly through its community: members recommend it, answer each other’s questions and help decide what it makes next. Shopify defines it as a marketing strategy that prioritizes the opinions of customers to influence everything from product development to marketing.

Does community-led growth work for a small business?

It can, when customers share a real reason to meet and the owner can show up regularly. Small businesses have an advantage: the founder can know members by name. The published evidence is mostly case studies, so treat it as a strategy to test for a few quarters, not a guaranteed result.

What is an example of community-led growth?

LEGO Ideas is one: fans post set designs, and a design that reaches 10,000 supporters goes to LEGO review, so the community shapes the product line. Smaller examples reported by Shopify include Set Active, whose community votes on designs for two community collections a year.

How is community-led growth different from product-led growth?

In product-led growth the product itself wins and keeps customers, often through a free trial. In community-led growth the people around the product do that work, by recommending it, teaching each other and giving feedback. Many businesses use both, with the community helping people get value from the product.
