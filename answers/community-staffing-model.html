Community authority · staffing

What roles does a brand community need?

A community operating model usually needs clear ownership for strategy, day-to-day member experience, moderation and escalation, programming, content or communications, analytics, and executive decisions. One person may cover several roles at small scale, but the responsibilities still need explicit owners.

Direct answer

A community operating model usually needs clear ownership for strategy, day-to-day member experience, moderation and escalation, programming, content or communications, analytics, and executive decisions. One person may cover several roles at small scale, but the responsibilities still need explicit owners.

How to approach it

  1. List required responsibilities before job titles
  2. Assign one accountable owner for each responsibility
  3. Separate daily operations from executive decisions
  4. Define moderation and escalation coverage
  5. Revisit staffing as membership and complexity grow

Decision framework

DecisionWhat to evaluateFailure signal
StrategyPurpose, roadmap, executive alignment, and prioritizationNo one owns strategic tradeoffs
Member operationsOnboarding, support, moderation, recognition, and daily presenceDaily member needs are squeezed between other jobs
ProgrammingEvents, content, facilitation, partnerships, and follow-upProgramming is ad hoc and inconsistent
AnalyticsInstrumentation, reporting, insight synthesis, and experimentsMetrics are gathered but no one turns them into decisions

What strong execution looks like

  • Every recurring responsibility has one accountable owner.
  • Backup and escalation coverage are documented.
  • Executive sponsorship is separate from daily community operations.
  • Role scope changes with community maturity.
  • Outsourced and internal responsibilities fit together without gaps.

Common failure modes

  • Hiring a “community manager” before defining the job.
  • Expecting one person to own strategy, events, moderation, support, analytics, and growth indefinitely.
  • Leaving weekend or crisis moderation uncovered.
  • Giving responsibility without authority.
  • Adding headcount before fixing broken workflows.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • Which responsibilities require daily coverage?
  • Which decisions require executive authority?
  • Where is specialist expertise needed?
  • What needs backup coverage?
  • What work should be automated, outsourced, or eliminated?
