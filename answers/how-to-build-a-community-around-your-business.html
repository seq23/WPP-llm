How do you build a community around your business?

A business community is a group of customers, fans or peers who meet, talk and help each other around what a business does, in a space the business hosts. Researchers call the brand version a brand community: “a specialized, non-geographically bound community, based on a structured set of social relations among admirers of a brand” (Muniz and O’Guinn, 2001).

Quick answer

To build a community around your business, pick one purpose your customers already share, choose one place for them to gather, keep a regular rhythm of meetings or posts, and give members ways to help each other rather than only listen to you. The brand communities that last, such as the Harley Owners Group and LEGO Ideas, give members something to do together.

What this guide is best for: business owners and marketing leads who have customers or followers and want them to become a community that keeps coming back.

What is a brand community?

A brand community is a group of people connected to each other through a brand, not only to the brand itself.

  • The research definition: Albert Muniz and Thomas O’Guinn named the idea in the Journal of Consumer Research in 2001, describing a community built on “a structured set of social relations among admirers of a brand” (Muniz and O’Guinn, 2001).
  • The business view: Susan Fournier and Lara Lee, writing in Harvard Business Review in 2009, called it “a group of ardent consumers organized around the lifestyle, activities, and ethos of the brand” (HBR, April 2009).
  • Audience or community? An audience listens to you. A community talks to each other. The test West Peek Productions uses is whether people “participate, contribute, connect, and return” (West Peek Productions).

How do you build a community for your business, step by step?

Work through these in order; each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Name the shared purpose. Write one sentence on who the community is for and why they would meet each other, not just you. “Owners of small bakeries who want to sell wholesale” works; “fans of our brand” does not.
  2. Find your first members by hand. Invite your most engaged customers personally. A small group that talks beats a large list that does not.
  3. Choose one home. Pick the place your members already use (see the table below) and start there only.
  4. Set a rhythm. A monthly meetup, a weekly thread or a quarterly event. People return to a habit, not to an announcement.
  5. Give members jobs. Ask them to answer questions, host a session, test a product or vote on a decision, the way LEGO Ideas and Set Active do.
  6. Connect people to each other. Introduce members by name and interest. Events that end without a next step, and members who rarely connect with one another, are two of the signs West Peek Productions looks for when a community is not compounding (West Peek Productions).
  7. Measure whether people come back and change one thing each cycle.

How do you know your community is working?

A community is working when members come back and talk to each other without being chased.

  • Return rate: how many members from last month took part again this month
  • Member-to-member replies: answers that came from members, not from your staff
  • Contributions: posts, ideas, hosted sessions or reviews members created
  • Referrals: new members or customers who came through an existing member
  • Business value you can name: questions that never reached support, product ideas you used, customers who stayed

Deciding who runs all this is its own question: see community manager or community agency. To use the community as your growth engine, see what community-led growth is.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an audience and a community?

An audience listens to you. A community talks to each other. If people only read, watch or buy, you have an audience. If they answer each other’s questions, come back to meet the same people and help shape what you do, you have the start of a community.

How big does a business need to be to build a community?

There is no minimum size. A community starts with a handful of customers who share a reason to meet. Shopify’s examples include brands that began at farmers markets and kept meeting customers in person as they grew online. Start small and make the first group work before you scale it.

What are good brand community examples?

Well-documented examples include the Harley Owners Group, created by Harley-Davidson in 1983 with local chapters at dealers; LEGO Ideas, where fan designs that reach 10,000 supporters go to LEGO review; and Sephora’s Beauty Insider Community, where members ask and answer beauty questions.

How long does it take to build a business community?

Plan in quarters, not weeks. The first 90 days usually go to finding the shared purpose, a first group of members and a regular rhythm of meetings or posts. Whether people come back without being chased is the sign it is working, and that takes several cycles of your rhythm to see.
