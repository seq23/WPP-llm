Direct answer

How discovery, onboarding, activation, participation, contribution, recognition, retention, re-engagement, and exit fit together.

What this page recommends

  1. Map how people discover and decide to join
  2. Design onboarding around first value
  3. Create participation and contribution paths
  4. Recognize members and deepen relationships
  5. Re-engage or offboard with clear expectations

Decision framework

DecisionWhat to evaluateFailure signal
Discovery and joinWhy the right member discovers and chooses the communityAcquisition targets anyone rather than qualified members
Onboarding and activationPath from entry to first meaningful valueThe first experience is administrative
Participation and contributionWays to consume, connect, contribute, and leadOnly the brand creates value
Retention and exitReasons to stay, re-engage, graduate, or leave wellInactive members remain unmanaged indefinitely

What strong execution looks like

  • Each lifecycle stage has a clear member job and operator goal.
  • Transitions between stages are measurable.
  • Contribution paths grow with trust and expertise.
  • Recognition supports identity and value.
  • Exit and re-engagement are designed rather than accidental.

Common failure modes

  • Optimizing join conversion without onboarding.
  • Treating all members as equally mature.
  • Offering no path from consumer to contributor.
  • Using generic re-engagement messages.
  • Ignoring healthy graduation or exit.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • How does the right person discover the community?
  • What is the first meaningful action after joining?
  • How can members contribute more over time?
  • Which signals show a member is stuck?
  • What does a healthy exit or graduation look like?
