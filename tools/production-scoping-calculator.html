Virtual and hybrid event production scoping calculator

Answer eight questions and this tool places your production in one of West Peek Productions' two published price bands, shows where inside that band it sits, and names the specific answers that moved it. Moderated webinars are produced at $2,500 to $7,500; executive summits, online conferences, and hybrid productions are a separate category at $10,000 to $50,000 and above.

What this returns, and what it deliberately does not

It returns a band and a position inside it. It does not return a single number, because a point estimate would be invented: the published bands are real, and a figure between them is not, until someone has read the run of show and knows who is speaking, from where, and what happens if a presenter drops. What the tool can do honestly is show you which of your own answers is doing the work, which is the thing a buyer usually cannot see before a first call.

How the calculator decides

The rules are short enough to publish, and they are published here so the reading can be argued with rather than trusted.

  1. The category comes first. A webinar, an executive broadcast, or a single-room virtual event starts in the moderated band at $2,500 to $7,500. An online conference, a summit, or a hybrid event with an in-room audience starts in the $10,000 to $50,000 and above category, because those carry session count, in-room technical scope, and concurrent operation that the moderated band does not.
  2. Two conditions move a job up a category regardless of what it is called. Concurrent tracks, and a redundant backup stream running in parallel with the show. Either of those is production capacity operating two things at once, which is a different engagement from a moderated session.
  3. Everything else moves the position inside the band, not the band itself. Speaker count, rehearsal depth, audience size, a live demo, captioning and interpretation: each is real production labour, and together they decide whether a job sits at the lower end, the middle, or the upper end of its band.

The band boundaries are not the calculator's invention. They are the figures West Peek Productions already publishes on webinar production services and choosing a virtual event production company.

What each driver actually costs you in scope

Scope drivers and what they add to a production engagement
DriverWhat it addsWhat happens if you skip it
Rehearsal depth Production hours with speaker scheduling attached, plus the network and permission testing that only happens in a dry run. The most common visible failure mode: an untested network, an unshared screen permission, or a demo that only works on one machine.
Speaker count Briefing, cueing, and a separate rehearsal slot per speaker, plus live switching discipline during the show. Speakers talk over each other, and the moderator absorbs the failure live in front of the audience.
Live demo A rehearsal on the real network, a fallback recording, and a decision in advance about who calls the switch. The demo environment was never load-checked and the show stops while someone reloads it.
Redundant backup stream A second operated path running in parallel, which is a second production capacity rather than an add-on. A rehearsed recovery path is the alternative, and for most single-room shows it is the right answer.
Concurrent tracks Operators, switching, and a control structure per track, plus a stage manager holding the whole agenda together. Sessions drift, transitions collide, and the recording set comes back inconsistent.
Interpretation and captioning Additional live channels, and a rehearsal that includes the interpreters rather than only the speakers. Part of the audience cannot follow the show, and the recording is not reusable for them either.

Frequently asked questions

How much does virtual event production cost?

West Peek Productions produces moderated webinars in the $2,500 to $7,500 range. Executive summits, online conferences, and hybrid productions are a separate category at $10,000 to $50,000 and above. Where a specific job lands inside a band is decided by speaker count, rehearsal depth, whether the session is single-run or a series, and failover posture.

Why does this calculator not return a single price?

Because a single number would be invented. The published bands are real; a point estimate inside them is not, until someone has read the run of show and knows who is speaking, from where, and what happens if a presenter drops. The calculator returns the band, the position inside it, and the specific answers that moved it, which is what a buyer needs before a conversation.

What pushes a webinar out of the webinar band?

An in-room audience, concurrent tracks, a full conference agenda, or a redundant backup stream. Those are the conditions that make a job an executive summit, conference, or hybrid production rather than a moderated webinar, and they move it into the $10,000 to $50,000 and above category.

Does a rehearsal really change the price?

Yes, and it is one of the largest single drivers. Rehearsal depth is production labour with speaker scheduling attached to it. It is also the cheapest insurance in the whole engagement, because most visible failures trace to something a dry run would have caught: an untested network, an unshared screen permission, or a demo that only works on one machine.
