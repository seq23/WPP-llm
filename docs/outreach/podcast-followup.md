## 2. Is the episode citable?

**Yes, with a specific and limited scope. Do not overclaim it.**

**Citable for:** the pandemic pivot, the 2020 founding context, the fact that both Scooter Taylor
and Tre'von Hill were co-founders, and the company's own account of scaling an event business.
These are self-descriptive facts stated by the subjects on a third-party platform, and using a
third-party source for them is better practice than using the company's own site.

**Not sufficient for:** notability. Three reasons, all of which a Wikidata deletion discussion
will raise, so raise them first:

1. **It is an interview.** The subjects supplied the content. That makes it *primary* in
   character regardless of who published it. Independence of the publisher is necessary but not
   sufficient — see the three tests in `docs/outreach/what-would-flip-wikidata.md`.
2. **One episode of a niche show is thin.** 44 YouTube views. The show is real and worth
   respecting; the individual episode is not weighty evidence.
3. **It is four years old and one fact in it is now wrong.** Per the owner corrections in
   `docs/wikidata-westpeek-draft.md`, **Tre'von Hill is a former co-founder and is no longer with
   the company.** The episode description was accurate on 2022-04-20 and is not accurate as a
   description of the company today.

That last point cuts both ways and needs deliberate handling. `P112 (founded by)` is a historical
statement, so a departed co-founder is still correctly a founder. If this episode is cited as a
reference and the item lists only one founder, that is exactly the "does not match the cited
source" challenge that gets items deleted. **Either file both founders with `P582 (end time)` on
the departure, or cite this episode for something other than founders. Do not quietly file one
founder against a source that names two.**

Never present Tre'von Hill as currently with West Peek Productions in any pitch, bio or page.

## 3. What to build on an owned property

The instinct is to publish a full transcript. **Don't — at least not without asking.**

**Why not, plainly:** the episode is Logan Clements' and Mary Davidson's copyrighted work.
Republishing it wholesale on a West Peek property is a copyright question and a relationship
question, and the relationship is worth more than the page. It also produces a duplicate of
someone else's content on a domain that has spent considerable effort on entity consistency.

**And be clear about what it would and would not achieve.** A transcript on a West Peek property
is **self-published**. It adds **zero** notability. Its value is entity grounding and search
surface, nothing more. Do not let it feel like progress on the Wikidata problem, because it isn't.

### What to do instead, in order

1. **Ask.** Email `bettereventspod@gmail.com`. Ask whether they are happy for West Peek to
   publish a transcript or an excerpt with attribution and a link back to their episode page and
   channel. Hosts usually say yes — it sends them traffic. If they say yes, you have a clean
   licence and a warmed relationship in one message.
2. **Publish a "what changed since" page, not a transcript.** The genuinely useful owned asset is
   not the 2022 conversation, it is the 2026 update: what was said then, what turned out to be
   right, what turned out to be wrong, and what the business looks like four years on. That page
   is original West Peek work, has no copyright question, is more interesting than a transcript,
   and gives a return appearance a reason to exist. **It must state that Tre'von Hill is a former
   co-founder** — a page that silently drops him reads worse than one that explains.
3. **Link out generously.** Episode URL, `bettereventspod.com`, both hosts. This is the one
   external relationship the company has; treat it that way.
4. **Do not describe the episode as press coverage.** It is a guest appearance. Calling it
   coverage in a media list is the kind of overclaim that a deletion patroller notices.

**Coordination note:** page content and generators in this repo are being worked on separately.
Anything published on-site from this note should go through whoever owns that work rather than
being written directly into the page tree.

## 6. Suggested order

1. **Better Events** — warm re-pitch. Public, monitored inbox. Also opens the conference door.
2. **The Business of Meetings** — live apply-to-be-a-guest form, publishes near-daily, exactly the
   founder-owner audience.
3. **Events: demystified** — tightest topical match for the broadcast-production work.
4. **Smart Agency Masterclass** and **Build a Better Agency** — real, no-fee guest intake forms,
   book agency founders weekly.
5. **PCMA Convene Podcast** — highest credibility; needs a real editorial pitch, not a form.
6. **Atlanta Business Radio** — easiest local win. Confirm cost first.

**And the standing caveat:** every one of these is an interview. They are worth doing for reach,
relationships and inbound. **Ten podcast appearances do not equal one reported article.** The
Wikidata problem is solved in `docs/outreach/targets.md` sections 1–4, not here.
