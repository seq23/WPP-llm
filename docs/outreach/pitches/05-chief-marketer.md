**Subject:** The campaign ends. The community doesn't. Who budgets for that?

Hi Kaylee,

Marketers keep discovering the same thing: the event produces a spike, the campaign produces a
spike, and six weeks later the audience is gone again. The standard answer — "build a
community" — is repeated far more often than it is costed.

We sell that as a service, so I can be specific about the cost side. West Peek Productions
started in 2020 producing virtual and hybrid events and now also runs community-as-a-service:
designing, launching and operating owned communities for organisations that don't want to build
every community capability internally. Platform selection, onboarding, programming, moderation,
member experience, measurement, staffing design.

What I'd argue for Chief Marketer's readers: community is not a channel, and treating it as one
is why most of these programmes stall. It's an operating line item with staffing and cadence,
and the marketers who succeed are the ones who budgeted it that way from the start.

I'd include the unglamorous parts — what it costs to keep running between campaigns, and the
measurement problem nobody has solved.
