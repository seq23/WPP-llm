What this topic covers

Presentation storytelling sits inside the storytelling and narrative pillar of this library. Presentation storytelling is treated here as a decision with an owner and a date attached, not as a subject to read about: every page below names what has to be settled, what evidence the call should rest on, and what the early warning looks like if it is going wrong.

Two people searching the same phrase rarely need the same page. Someone still deciding whether to hire out at all needs a different page from someone with a shortlist and a budget, which is why the 19 pages are grouped by decision below rather than listed alphabetically.

How to use this hub

  1. Name the decision. Choosing a partner, sizing the cost, running the work, or catching a failure - the section headings match those four.
  2. Read the one page that matches it. Each page is written to settle one question, so reading three is usually a sign the decision is not yet framed.
  3. Take the checklist into the meeting. The templates section holds the artefacts meant to leave this site: briefs, vendor questions, and checklists.
